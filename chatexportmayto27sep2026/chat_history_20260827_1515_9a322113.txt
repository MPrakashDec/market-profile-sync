---

### 1. Underlying Reference: Spot vs. Synthetic Future
* **Standard Institutional Black-Scholes (Our default):** Uses the **Synthetic Future / Forward Price ($F$)** derived from Put-Call parity ($F = K + C - P \approx 24,243.39$), which eliminates the cash-futures premium distortion.
* **StockMock:** Uses the **NIFTY Cash Spot Index ($S$)** (e.g., ~24,200.00). When Call options have futures premium baked into their market price, computing IV against Cash Spot elevates the Call IV (e.g., pushing it from ~8.4% to **~11.5% - 13%**).

---

### 2. Time to Expiry ($T$): Calendar Days vs. Trading Days
* **Standard Black-Scholes:** $T = \frac{\text{Calendar Days}}{365} = \frac{5.02}{365} \approx 0.01375 \text{ years}$.
* **StockMock Convention:** Many backtesting platforms like StockMock count only **Trading Days / 252** (or trading minutes / $(250 \times 375)$).
  * Since Thursday to Tuesday has only **3 trading days** (skipping Saturday & Sunday), $T_{\text{trading}} = \frac{3.08}{252} \approx 0.01222 \text{ years}$.
  * Because $T$ is smaller in the denominator ($\sigma \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}$), the resulting IV is **higher**.

---

### 3. Risk-Free Interest Rate ($r$)
* **Standard Model:** Uses the current RBI Repo / MIBOR rate ($r = 6.5\%$).
* **StockMock:** Typically sets $r = 0\%$ (or in some legacy modules, a fixed $r = 10\%$).

---

### 4. DTE Discretization (Full Days vs. Exact Minutes)
* Some backtesters round $DTE$ to discrete whole numbers (e.g., $DTE = 5$ or $DTE = 4$) rather than exact second-level continuous compounding.
